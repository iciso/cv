💊 The Drug of Choice & Resistance Evolution Game 🦠

One population. One treatment decision. Many possible consequences. Can you choose wisely and protect the microbial ecosystem?

Welcome to the laboratory

🎯 Your mission

You receive a laboratory report, just as in daily practice: organism, zone diameters or MICs, and S/I/R interpretations. Identify the organism, pick the single Drug of Choice from the list, and observe what happens to susceptible organisms, resistant organisms and normal flora.

Important principle: Antimicrobial resistance is not usually created because a bacterium consciously learns to resist a drug. Resistant organisms may already exist, may acquire resistance mechanisms, and can become more common when susceptible organisms are eliminated.

This is an educational simulation using fictional patients, illustrative laboratory values and illustrative population units. It is not a diagnostic or prescribing tool. Do not use its choices, settings or numerical results for patient treatment.

Step 5 • Learning by reflection

💡 What have we learned?

1. Selection, not intention

Antimicrobial exposure can remove susceptible organisms while resistant organisms survive and become a larger proportion.

2. Resistance can pre-exist

A population may contain a small resistant subpopulation even before treatment begins.

3. Normal flora matters

Antimicrobials can disturb beneficial microbial communities. This may allow resistant organisms or opportunistic microbes to expand.

4. Correct organism, correct treatment

Antibiotics do not treat viral or fungal infections. Antivirals and antifungals have different targets.

Discussion points

  • Was the organism bacterial, viral or fungal?
  • Was the specimen appropriate and clinically meaningful?
