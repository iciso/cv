🏡 Build a Better Village 🌱

A community development and resource-prioritisation game. Can your village build a better future with limited resources?

Your mission

One village. Many needs. One shared future.

Welcome to Vasanthapuram, a fictional village. Its people want better health, education, water, sanitation, roads and livelihoods. But the available development budget is limited.

Your task is not simply to spend money. Your task is to understand the community, discover its strengths, agree on priorities and plan realistic action together.

Remember: In CLCP, the community is the main actor. A facilitator helps people listen, appreciate, learn and decide. The facilitator does not arrive with all the answers.

Stage 1 of 5 • Building relationships

1. Who are we?

Before discussing projects, bring the community together. Every person has experiences, abilities and something valuable to contribute.

👩 Women and self-help groups

Knowledge of families, savings, nutrition and local needs.

👨‍🌾 Farmers and workers

Knowledge of land, water, work, seasons and local production.

👩‍🎓 Young people

Ideas, energy, digital skills and aspirations for the future.

👵 Elders and experienced residents

Community history, local knowledge and relationships.

SALT reflection: Appreciate people's contributions. Listen before suggesting solutions. Create a safe space where everyone can speak.
Stage 2 of 5 • Shared vision

2. Where do we want to go?

Invite people to imagine the village they would love to live in. Begin with hopes and possibilities—not a list of complaints.

Imagine it is five years from today. The village has made wonderful progress. What has changed in everyday life?

Which outcomes matter most to the community?

Choose up to three. There is no correct answer.

CLCP insight: A shared dream gives direction. It should belong to the people—not only to officials or outside experts.

Stage 4 of 5 • Understanding the situation

4. Where are we now?

Now compare the community's dream with its present reality. Listen to different groups. Look at who is affected, how seriously, and what is already working.

💧 Drinking water

Some households travel far for water. The quality and reliability of supply vary.

🚻 Sanitation

Drainage is poor in some lanes. Waste disposal needs improvement.

🩺 Health

The clinic is far away. Preventive care and timely access are concerns.

📚 Education

The school needs improvements. Some children struggle to attend regularly.

🛣️ Roads

Some roads become difficult to use during the rainy season.

🌱 Livelihoods

Families need more reliable income and opportunities for local work.

CLCP insight: A village walk, community conversation, mapping exercise and shared observation can reveal realities that outsiders may not see.

Learning corner

What should we remember?

🤝 Participation

People affected by a decision should have a meaningful voice in making it.

💎 Start with strengths

Recognise local knowledge, skills, relationships and resources—not only problems.

⚖️ Prioritisation

Limited resources require transparent discussion about needs, benefits and trade-offs.

🔗 Interconnection

Health, water, sanitation, education, roads and livelihoods influence one another.

🧭 Local ownership

Community members should help decide, act, maintain and review their initiatives.

🔄 Learning cycle

Act, reflect, learn, share and decide what to do next. The cycle continues.

Facilitator's note

How to use this game in a CLCP session

  1. Invite small groups to play together. Encourage different voices.
  2. Pause at each stage. Ask participants to explain their thinking.
  3. Ask whether the selected projects came from the community's dream and assessment.
  4. Compare two groups' budgets. Ask why their priorities differ.
  5. Discuss what can be started using existing local strengths.
  6. Connect the game to a real community conversation, not just a paper exercise.

This game is a learning aid. Real community planning must use local evidence, inclusive participation, feasibility assessment and the appropriate local decision-making processes.
