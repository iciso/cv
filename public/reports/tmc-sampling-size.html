“BIGGER helps; BETTER selection matters.”

A large biased sample can be confidently wrong. Sampling design comes before sample size.

Field note: A sample is a small window onto a population—not the population itself.

Quick memory hooks

SAMPLE

Select fairly • Ask a clear question • Mix in all groups • Plan size • Look for bias • Explain uncertainty.

“More people, less wobble—unless the doorway is wrong.”

Increasing n usually reduces random fluctuation. It does not remove selection bias.

“Random is not haphazard.”

A probability sample uses a planned method so eligible people have a known chance of selection. Convenience sampling just takes whoever is easiest to reach.

“Clinic ≠ community.”

People attending a clinic may be sicker or more health-conscious than those who do not attend. Clinic data answer clinic questions—not automatically village questions.

What just happened?

Run a survey to get a short interpretation linked to your choices.

Why do estimates change between surveys?

Different random samples include different people. This is sampling variability. With small samples, a few people can move the percentage a lot.

Why can a large survey still be wrong?

If the selection process systematically misses or over-represents a group, the estimate can be biased. A bigger sample may make that biased estimate more precise without making it representative.

What does “±5 percentage points” mean here?

It is a simple visual learning target, not a formal confidence interval. Real uncertainty depends on design, weighting, non-response, clustering and other factors.
