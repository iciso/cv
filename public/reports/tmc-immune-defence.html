5. 🧠 Quick Revision Cards

What distinguishes innate from adaptive immunity?

Innate immunity provides rapid, non-specific recognition via pattern recognition receptors (PRRs). Adaptive immunity relies on somatic recombination of antigen receptors (TCR/BCR), producing high specificity and memory, but takes time to deploy initially.

What is an antigen vs. an antibody?

An antigen is a molecule or structure capable of binding specifically to immune components. An antibody is a Y-shaped immunoglobulin produced by plasma cells that binds specific antigenic epitopes.

How does vaccination induce immunological memory without illness?

Vaccines present non-pathogenic antigens (attenuated, inactivated, subunit, or mRNA encoded) to naive lymphocytes. This stimulates clonal expansion and memory cell formation without causing disease complications.

Why are booster doses sometimes required?

Antibody titers and memory cell populations can decline over time (waning immunity). Additionally, viral antigenic drift may require updated antigenic stimulation to maintain efficacy.

How should clinicians address vaccine hesitancy respectfully?

Acknowledge patient concerns empathetically, clarify specific doubts without jargon, cite reliable public health data (WHO/CDC), and transparently explain risk versus benefit profiles.
