🌍 The Community Participation Simulator

Can you accompany a community without taking over? Explore SALT, practise the CLCP learning cycle, and discover how local strengths can lead to community-owned action.

🎯 Your mission

You are accompanying a community concerned about children missing school and families struggling to support their education.

Some parents know how to help. Some young people have ideas. A few people speak often, while others remain quiet. The community has strengths, but has not yet agreed on what to do together.

Your role: help people connect, discover strengths, agree on a shared direction, take action, and learn from experience.

Remember: this is a learning model, not a prediction of how real people will behave. The indicators illustrate possible effects of facilitation choices.

🧠 SALT pocket guide

S — Stimulate / Support

Encourage people to express themselves, connect, and begin acting. Support the community's own response rather than supplying every answer.

Ask: “What has helped us come together before?”

A — Appreciate / Accompany

Notice strengths, effort, experience, and what is already working. Accompany people through progress and setbacks.

Ask: “What are we already doing well?”

L — Listen / Learn / Link

Listen with curiosity, learn from community experience, and connect people so they can learn from one another.

Ask: “What have we learned, and who else might learn from it?”

T — Transfer / Team / Trust / Transform

Help useful experience travel between people and communities, build teamwork and trust, and support change that people own.

Ask: “Who else could adapt this learning to their situation?”

🔄 The CLCP learning cycle

Move through these seven stages. In real community life, the process is cyclical: reflection leads to another round of learning and action.

💡 Quick revision: frequently asked questions

Is participation the same as attendance?

No. People may attend a meeting without influencing the decisions. Participation means having opportunities to express experience, contribute ideas, and shape action.

Who owns the solution?

The community. A facilitator creates space for discussion, reflection, learning, and action; the facilitator should not replace community decision-making.

Why start with strengths?

Recognising existing capacities helps people see that they can already take action and build on what they have learned from experience.

Does SALT mean ignoring problems?

No. An appreciative approach does not require ignoring difficulties. It helps people face challenges while also identifying capacities and useful experience.

Is there a final endpoint to CLCP?

No. The learning cycle continues. Each round of action and reflection can inform the next.

What if the community's first action fails?

Failure or setbacks can become material for reflection. Ask what happened, what was learned, and what the community wants to try next.

🎉 Journey reflection

Discuss with your learning group

  1. When were you tempted to provide the answer instead of asking a question?
  2. Whose voice might still be missing from the discussion?
  3. What can the community learn from its own experience?
  4. How could another community adapt this learning without simply copying it?
